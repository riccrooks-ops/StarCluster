# Auxiliary Component Foundation and Availability Gating v0.1

## Status

Checkpoint 43 candidate foundation. This document changes no accepted combat mechanic and promotes no Auxiliary component as a production player choice.

## Installation classes

Star Cluster uses three installation classes.

1. **Dedicated Core Integration** - baseline systems such as the normal hull, armor, shield generator, main reactor, drives, standard sensor/command systems, and other explicitly core components use dedicated hull integration channels instead of generic Auxiliary Capacity.
2. **Weapon Bay** - direct-fire batteries, launchers, and other primary attack packages consume explicit Weapon Bay capacity.
3. **Auxiliary Capacity** - optional defensive, support, utility, logistics, and specialist modules compete for generic Auxiliary Capacity.

The classification answers *where a component is installed*. It does not make any component free.

## Core does not mean free

A Dedicated Core component may avoid generic Auxiliary Capacity while still imposing construction or refit cost, hull integration and service-access requirements, Tactical Power, fuel, ammunition, crew and maintenance burden, technology and compatibility requirements, Critical Exposure, and damage consequences. A second copy is not automatically core. It needs an explicit dedicated mount or consumes the appropriate Weapon Bay or Auxiliary Capacity.

The stripped TL1 calibration cruiser may continue to use zero Auxiliary Capacity when a study needs strict isolation. That fixture is not the normal player-ship loadout. Ordinary player hulls are expected to have a small baseline AUX allowance; the exact value is not promoted in Checkpoint 43. Higher Hull TL may expand capacity. The TL3 hull promise is therefore an **Auxiliary Capacity expansion milestone**, not necessarily the first existence of AUX capacity.

## No visible Auxiliary research tree

Standard Auxiliary items remain individual components tied to the broad research categories already visible to the player. Every AUX family carries:

- a stable component-family ID and candidate Item TL;
- one primary research category and normally no more than two support floors;
- minimum Hull TL, Auxiliary Capacity cost, compatibility requirements, and stacking limits;
- Tactical Power, finite-store, activation, or task behavior;
- an explicit damage profile when independently damageable; and
- a candidate first standard-player availability floor.

Alien, Precursor, and rare rule-breaking items remain outside this standard catalog. A standard player cloak is deliberately excluded.

## Availability gate

The earliest candidate review point is the maximum of:

`candidate Item TL, primary research floor, every support floor, minimum Hull TL, compatibility and capacity legality, and the balance-entry gate`

This is an earliest *review* point, not automatic production availability. All 27 catalog entries remain `candidate_only` and `not_promoted`.

A TL1 calibration fixture proves that a mechanic can execute. It does not prove that the same capability is a healthy or legal standard TL1 player choice.

## Performance-first TL placement

When an AUX component produces an overpowered advantage at its proposed entry TL, the first correction is to move its candidate starting TL upward. Preserve the component's established mechanical identity where possible. Change the mechanic only when the component remains unhealthy at the later floor or when the mechanic itself is unclear, unfun, or internally inconsistent.

The strictest entry gates apply to capabilities that create renewable power, flat damage prevention, extra action capacity, movement denial, strategic-economy multipliers, or cooperative stacking. These functions can cross thresholds or compound faster than ordinary one-TL statistical refinement.

## Candidate floor examples

- TL1 candidates are primarily conventional, finite, passive, or bounded: kinetic/AMM PDS, APU, Combat Battery, Shield Battery, ablative armor, Active Sensors, basic laboratories/mining/cargo, and magazine expansions.
- TL2 candidates introduce stronger efficiency, information, repair, burst-power, or network effects: Energy PDS, Auxiliary Reactor, Power Capacitor, Shield Booster, Power Stabilizer, EvM, ECM/ECCM, Repair Drones, Fabrication, and Communications Relay.
- TL3 candidates introduce qualitative control or flat-protection milestones: Shield Hardener, Energized Armor Controller, Tractor Projector, and Auxiliary Hangar Bay.

These floors are hypotheses. They may move upward after isolated and integrated balance studies. They may not move downward or become production values without an explicit later acceptance checkpoint.

## Required later evidence

High-risk families need isolated studies first, then representative complete-loadout comparisons. Evidence must report the benefit, Tactical Power or finite-store burden, capacity opportunity cost, stacking behavior, interaction with counters, pacing, and whether the component becomes a compulsory choice. A component that is consistently mandatory has failed the situational-viability objective even when its raw win rate appears reasonable.
