# TL2 Dynamic Weapon-Choice Confirmation v0.1

## Purpose

Checkpoint 46 tests whether Kinetic, Energy, and Missile remain credible same-TL choices when each family uses its established Preferred Range doctrine. It does not tune the families toward identical outcomes.

## Profiles

- **Leading provisional reference:** `tl2-r45-armor-step-conservative-direct-fire`
- **Structural control:** `tl2-r45-hull-step-conservative-direct-fire`

Both profiles preserve the same propulsion, power/logistics, fire-control, guidance, and weapon-family vectors. They differ only in the one-point structural progression: Armor Integrity versus Hull. Neither is production data.

## Study grid

The primary study contains exactly 108 variants.

- 36 fixed mutual-envelope controls: 2 profiles x 3 unordered family pairs x 2 orientations x ranges 2-4.
- 72 dynamic Preferred Range lanes: 2 profiles x 3 unordered family pairs x 2 orientations x starting ranges 1-6.

Each orientation pair shares a comparison-group seed. Dynamic lanes use no disengagement objective.

## Interpretation

Fixed controls describe close-to-middle combat when both families begin in a mutually legal envelope. Dynamic lanes ask whether Kinetic can close and whether Energy or Missile can preserve useful standoff distance. Starting-range, pooled pairwise, role-expression, and orientation reports must be reviewed together.

A 75 percent share at one starting range is a warning, not an automatic failure. Broad dominance requires a strong pooled edge across at least four starting ranges. A family is potentially irrelevant when it has no starting range with at least a 25 percent decisive share. All such signals are non-blocking.

## Promotion boundary

Checkpoint 46 promotes no profile or weapon value automatically. A later decision may adopt the leading profile only if the complete evidence shows meaningful choices and acceptable game feel without characteristic convergence.
