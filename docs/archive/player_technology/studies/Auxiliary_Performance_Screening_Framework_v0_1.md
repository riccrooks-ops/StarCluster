# Auxiliary Performance Screening Framework v0.1

## Purpose

Checkpoint 48 begins quantitative screening of combat-evaluable Auxiliary components after promotion of the accepted TL2 core combat package. The objective is not equal results. It is to identify distinctive, credible choices and detect broad compulsory selection, practical irrelevance, premature capability floors, or unhealthy stacking risks.

## Legal configuration rule

Every standard matrix ship installs exactly one legal Auxiliary component.

- TL1 screening capacity: 1 Auxiliary Capacity.
- TL2 screening integration capacity: 2 Auxiliary Capacity.
- Checkpoint 48 still installs only one component per ship so each component's contribution remains attributable.
- A capacity-2 Auxiliary Reactor is therefore testable at TL2 without mixing it with another component.

The TL2 capacity value is an initial screening context, not permission to infer that every future TL2 hull or loadout must use two separate components. Multi-component legal loadouts and stacking are deferred to a dedicated follow-on study.

Same-AUX mirrors establish ordinary side and random-stream neutrality. Cross-AUX comparisons reveal role expression, counters, and broad dominance. Cross-TL comparisons show whether an AUX choice erases or excessively amplifies the accepted one-TL ship advantage.

## Counterfactual no-AUX diagnostics

No-AUX ships are not legal standard configurations. The empty slot is used only to estimate absolute component contribution.

- Each legal AUX is tested against an otherwise identical empty-slot ship in both orientations and all three weapon contexts.
- Empty-slot mirrors validate the counterfactual baseline.
- These 132 variants are excluded from standard choice balance, promotion gates, and legal aggregate reports.

## Combat-evaluable subset

Checkpoint 48 screens only families whose current mechanics can be represented faithfully in the integrated duel simulator. Strategic, economic, sensor, communications, hangar, and incomplete EW families remain deferred rather than being treated as zero-value combat choices.

TL1 legal profiles: Kinetic PDS, Anti-Missile Missile PDS, Combat Battery, Shield Battery, Ablative Armor, Kinetic Magazine Expansion, and Missile Magazine Expansion.

TL2 legal profiles add Energy PDS, Auxiliary Reactor, Power Capacitor, Shield Booster, Shield Power Stabilizer, Evasive Maneuver System, and Repair Drone Bay.

All values remain candidate screening values until a later decision promotes them.

Damage coupling remains active in the integrated screen. Dedicated PDS, Evasive Maneuver, and Auxiliary Reactor components use their existing condition behavior. Batteries, capacitors, shield-support modules, and repair drones use a damageable selected-AUX component and cease providing active benefits when Disabled or Destroyed. Ablative armor and magazine expansions are represented through their armor layer or expanded storage component rather than an extra damage-sponging component.

## Interpretation

Exact parity is nonblocking. A healthy component may be strongly favored in its intended weapon, range, threat, or endurance context. Warning conditions include:

- Broad success across most weapon contexts and opponents.
- A choice becoming rationally compulsory despite its capacity and Tactical Power costs.
- Another legal option becoming practically irrelevant.
- A TL1 AUX negating the accepted TL2 progression broadly.
- A TL2 AUX amplifying the normal TL step into overwhelming dominance.
- A high-impact capability appearing before the rest of the design space can counter it.

When a component is too strong, first confirm the pattern, then examine capacity, Tactical Power, ammunition, charges, reliability, and counters. If the capability is premature, raise its starting TL before weakening its established identity.

## Calibration doctrine

Same-family Kinetic, Energy, and Missile contexts isolate AUX effects without flattening weapon identity. Opponent-aware range control represents competent ideal use. Information-constrained production behavior is a later AI concern.
