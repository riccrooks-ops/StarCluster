# TL2 Defensive Micro-Refinement and Choice-Viability Study v0.1

## Purpose

Checkpoint 44 established that structure and shields drive most of the excessive integrated TL2 advantage. Checkpoint 45 tests smaller defensive increments while preserving propulsion, power/logistics, missile guidance, and weapon-family identity. It also repairs movement reporting so low-resolution withdrawal lanes cannot distort combat win-share summaries.

## Balance philosophy

The study does **not** seek equal results between every weapon family or an exact package percentage. It asks whether choices remain meaningful.

- Preserve identity first; tune strength second.
- Era-specific advantages are acceptable. Energy may lead early, Missiles in the middle, and Kinetic later.
- A family may be strongest in its preferred envelope without becoming compulsory everywhere.
- A family is unhealthy when it broadly dominates alternatives, lacks a meaningful tradeoff or counter, or makes another family irrelevant.
- Characteristics, costs, and starting/improvement TLs are all legitimate balance knobs.
- Numerical bands remain review aids rather than automatic promotion rules.

## Profiles

Nine non-promoted profiles are registered:

1. Exact TL1 null-vector control.
2. Original identity-package control.
3. Checkpoint 44 shield-tempered control.
4. Checkpoint 44 structure-tempered control.
5. Defense-neutral anchor: moderated fire control with TL1 Hull, Armor Integrity, and Shield Capacity.
6. Hull-step candidate: defense-neutral plus one Hull.
7. Armor-Integrity-step candidate: defense-neutral plus one Armor Integrity.
8. Hull-step plus conservative direct-fire accuracy progression.
9. Armor-step plus conservative direct-fire accuracy progression.

The conservative direct-fire probes change Kinetic and Energy accuracy from a +5 to a +3 TL2 increment while retaining their five-point relative accuracy separation, distinct damage, penetration, range, power, and ammunition identities. Missile guidance remains unchanged in this pass.

## Monte Carlo coverage

The study contains exactly **1,188 variants**:

- 648 paired fixed-range cross-TL variants: nine profiles, nine ordered family pairings, Ranges 2-5, and both orientations.
- 162 paired Preferred Range cross-TL variants.
- 81 valid pursuit-combat variants with the TL2 profile as Side A pursuer and disengagement disabled.
- 81 escape-control variants with the TL2 profile as Side B withdrawing ship and disengagement enabled.
- 216 same-TL identity variants for six selected profiles across all ordered family pairings and Ranges 2-5.

## Reporting corrections

Headline cross-TL results use pooled trial outcomes rather than an equal average of per-variant conditional percentages. Every aggregate reports:

- total trials;
- candidate wins and TL1 wins;
- mutual and unresolved outcomes;
- decisive-trial coverage;
- pooled conditional win share; and
- an explicit low-coverage classification.

Preferred Range, pursuit combat, and escape controls are reported separately. Escape controls never enter the ordinary combat aggregate. The prior equal-weight conditional average remains available only as a secondary diagnostic.

## Choice-viability diagnostics

Same-family progression is reported independently for Kinetic, Energy, and Missile. Same-TL cross-family dominance is evaluated only where both weapons can legally threaten at the initial fixed range. Reach-stress cases remain visible but do not define general dominance.

The initial warning signals are deliberately broad and non-blocking:

- more than 80 percent same-family TL2/TL1 progression;
- more than 75 percent mutually legal same-TL cross-family share;
- less than 25 percent mutually legal same-TL cross-family share; or
- insufficient decisive coverage.

These signals identify compulsory-choice or irrelevance risks. They do not require universal parity and do not promote or reject a profile automatically.

## Decision rule after results

Prefer the smallest profile that produces a believable TL2 feel, retains all three family roles, avoids universal dominance, resolves ordinary combat reliably, and leaves progression room for TL3. If two profiles are both reasonable, prefer the one with clearer identity and simpler values rather than the one numerically closest to a target percentage.
